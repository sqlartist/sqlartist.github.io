Due to my Chronic Cluster Headaches mentioned in the story “The Cloud Has No Mercy” [The Cloud Has No Mercy](/posts/the-cloud-has-no-mercy/), I was told after many years of devastating pain that I would require an operation and a device called a Deep Brain Stimulator installed as they had tried everything else open to them.

Deep Brain Stimulation (DBS) is an advanced, invasive surgical option reserved for patients with chronic cluster headaches who are entirely refractory (unresponsive) to standard medical therapies.

How the Procedure Works

Target Areas: Surgeons implant permanent electrodes into specific deep brain structures, most commonly the posterior hypothalamus or the ventral tegmental area (VTA), which mediate pain and are involved in the Neurobiology of cluster attacks.

The Device: Small holes are drilled into the skull to guide the electrodes into position. Wires connect these electrodes to a battery-powered pulse generator placed under the skin in the chest, like a heart pacemaker, which delivers regulated electrical impulses.

## The Study for DARPA

I was contacted through my Neurologist if I would be part of a study for the University of \[Redacted\] which was funded by DARPA, the USA’s Defence Advanced Research Projects Agency. They were looking for people with my exact Deep Brain Simulator to do some testing on.

I don’t really know why I said yes, but I did have some tough questions for them before I committed to anything.

I asked — is it a paid study? They said no it wasn’t paid but they would cover expenses. So, I said OK. *(I would not do well in an interrogation )*

**The Cyber-Vulnerability Fact**

In a terrifying twist of real-world history, the major medical manufacturers responsible for building these advanced thoracic IPG units (who also dominate the global heart pacemaker market) notoriously left glaring security holes in their systems and devices. For years, implantable devices and transmitters faced massive FDA cyber-security roll-outs because firmware lacked basic encryption. Historically, hard-coded master technician keys and default factory settings — colloquially mocked by white-hat hackers as equivalent to leaving the device password as **“Password123”** — left hundreds of thousands of hearts and neurological pacemakers vulnerable to remote signal interception.
